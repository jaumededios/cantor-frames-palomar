# Cantor measure frames — Palomar workspace

This repository is the small, standalone Comparator workspace for the main
result of:

> Jaume de Dios Pont, Lukas Liehr, and Mitchell A. Taylor,
> *Cantor measures with odd base do not admit Fourier frames*,
> [arXiv:2607.08656v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08656).

The result says that if `b > 1` is odd, then the `{0, 2}` base-`b` Cantor
measure does not admit a Fourier frame.

## What to audit

[`Challenge.lean`](Challenge.lean) is the complete human-auditable statement
surface. It imports only Mathlib and defines:

- the fair `{0, 2}` base-`b` Cantor measure;
- complex exponentials;
- frames and exponential systems; and
- the theorem `NoFourierFrameExists`.

[`Solution.lean`](Solution.lean) imports the proof from
[`jaumededios/Cantor_Measure_Frames`](https://github.com/jaumededios/Cantor_Measure_Frames)
at the immutable commit recorded in [`lakefile.toml`](lakefile.toml).
Comparator checks that the challenge and solution expose the same theorem and
that the proof uses only `propext`, `Quot.sound`, and `Classical.choice`.

## Statement fidelity

The public theorem deliberately uses an `ℕ`-indexed exponential family, as
does the Lean-facing statement displayed in Appendix 5 of the paper. This is
the accessible sequence formulation of a countable family, not a restriction
introduced by this wrapper.

The challenge uses `Bool` for a fair two-point digit space where the appendix
prints `Fin 2`. The explicit map `false ↦ 0`, `true ↦ 1` canonically identifies
the resulting product measures. The coding map, pushforward measure, complex
exponentials, almost-everywhere equality, and two-sided frame inequalities
otherwise follow Appendix 5 directly. The complete field-by-field account is
in [`formalization.yaml`](formalization.yaml).

## Provenance and review

The paper authors are Jaume de Dios Pont, Lukas Liehr, and Mitchell A. Taylor.
The final paper is human-written. The public Lean statement and its supporting
definitions are human-written as well: the authors rejected an initial
language-model draft and rewrote the statement surface into the published
version.

Language-model involvement was also mathematical. The project first explored a
positive frame construction; GPT-5.5 helped diagnose its failure through a
Bessel-bound obstruction, which suggested the obstruction in the final
negative proof, and helped simplify a normalized polynomial used there.
GPT-5.5 in Codex assisted the Lean formalization, while the internal Lean proof
files were generated by language models and are intended for kernel-checked
verification rather than as a reader-facing proof.
That is distinct from the human-written paper and public statement surface;
this repository does not claim line-by-line human review of the generated proof
code. For the full account, see the paper, Section 1.5 and Appendix 5.

Jaume de Dios Pont maintains the source formalization and is the responsible
maintainer for this wrapper.

This repository's `formalization.yaml` was written in part by Codex with
extensive human feedback. Jaume supplied substantive corrections and confirmed

## Literature context

The following is an automated Codex summary of Sections 1.2--1.4 of the source
paper, not an independent literature review or novelty assessment.

Jorgensen and Pedersen proved spectrality for the even-base Cantor measures.
For odd bases, the known absence of sufficiently large orthogonal exponential
families ruled out an exponential basis but left open the weaker possibility of
a Fourier frame. Strichartz asked this explicitly for the middle-third
measure. Non-spectrality alone could not answer the question: Lai and Wang
constructed non-spectral fractal measures with Fourier frames, while
Picioroaga and Weber constructed Parseval frames for the Cantor-4 measure.
Dutkay, Emami, and Lai found maximal-Beurling-dimension exponential Riesz
sequences for the middle-third measure, again short of a frame. The source
paper's theorem excludes Fourier frames for every odd base; together with the
even-base result, this gives the parity classification.

The precise citations and the relationship of each to the formalized result
are recorded in [`formalization.yaml`](formalization.yaml). See the paper for
the authors' full literature discussion and bibliography.

That metadata also identifies the `VibeProving/Strichartz-Problem`
development-history repository: its pinned tree is byte-for-byte the same
artifact as the proof dependency, not an unacknowledged second formalization.
